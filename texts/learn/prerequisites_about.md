## Risk-Free Interest Rate

The risk-free interest rate, also referred to as just the interest rate or the risk-free rate (we will use the latter), refers to the theoretical return of a risk-free investment. In practice the risk-free rate is often proxied as the interest/return on highly rated government bonds or US Treasury bills. One may argue that government debt is not risk-free however a rebuttal I've found entertaining goes along the lines of "if the \[US\] government defaults on its debt people will be running around the street with AKs". While this exaggeration is not strictly true and far milder examples of governments defaulting on their debt can be easily found in history I believes it encourages the appropriate lines of thought.

## Dividends

Dividends refers to cash or share payouts to investors for holding eligible shares of a company or units of other financial instruments. Dividends are typically paid out periodically. There are a few important dates related to dividends:
- The *announcement date* is the date which a managing body declares a round of dividends.
- The *ex-dividend date* is the date at which new shareholders are no longer entitled to the round of dividends. To be eligible for dividends a share must be held before the ex-dividend date.
- The *record date* is the date by which a managing body must determine which shareholders are eligible for the round of dividends.
- The *payment date*, as the name implies, is the date the round of dividends is paid to investors.

The dividend yield refers to the dividend payout as a percentage of the share price or trading price of the instrument.
