
# Some Prerequisites

We will now cover some prerequisite terms and concepts necessary to properly interact with and understand this project.

## Risk-Free Interest Rate

The risk-free interest rate, also referred to as just the interest rate or the risk-free rate (we will use the latter), refers to the theoretical return of a risk-free investment. In practice the risk-free rate is often proxied by the yield on highly rated government bonds or US Treasury bills. One may argue that government debt is not risk-free; however, a rebuttal I've found entertaining goes along the lines of "if the \[US\] government defaults on its debt people will be running around the street with AKs". While this is an exaggeration, and far milder examples of governments defaulting on their debt can be easily found in history, I believe it encourages the appropriate lines of thought.

## Dividends

Dividends refer to cash or share payouts to investors for holding eligible shares of a company or units of other financial instruments. Dividends are typically paid out periodically. There are a few important dates related to dividends:
- The *announcement date* is the date on which a managing body declares a round of dividends.
- The *ex-dividend date* is te date on which new shareholders are no longer entitled to the round of dividends. To be eligible for dividends, a share must be bought before the ex-dividend date.
- The *record date* is the date on which a managing body examines its registry of shareholders and records who will receive the round of dividends.
- The *payment date*, as the name implies, is the date the round of dividends is paid to investors.

The dividend yield refers to the dividend payout as a percentage of the share price or trading price of the instrument. Note that the dividend yield can refer to either the annual dividend payout as a percentage of the share price or the payout of a single dividend period as a percentage of the share price.
